# Learning Log

## Sprint planning

For this sprint we picked 13 goals that all build one loop: remind, confirm, record, flag and alert. We picked this loop because it is the main safety feature. Harold went to hospital after taking a double dose of his blood thinner (T2:14; F2:7), and all three stakeholders said dose safety matters most (T1:44; T2:45; T3:65). G-4, G-7 and G-8 (reminder, one-tap confirm, save the time) are the base, because everything else needs those records. G-17 and G-43 (show the last-taken time, and "I'm not sure") help stop another double dose. G-38, G-22, G-24 and G-25 let Dr. Nguyen set a window and risk level for each medication, so the app can flag a dose and choose between an urgent alert and a simple notice to Harold. G-34, G-33 and G-42 make sure Rachel isn't alerted when Harold is only a bit late, and that she only sees what Harold agrees to. G-36 makes the text and buttons big enough for Harold. This loop is also user-facing (Harold's tablet view, Rachel's alert view, Dr. Nguyen's settings view) and there is a lot we don't know yet. Each person defines "missed" differently, urgency depends on the medication, and Rachel's access depends on consent. So V1 was a way to test if per-medication windows, two urgency levels and the consent tiers actually work.

Some goals overlapped, so we combined them. All three stakeholders asked for one-tap confirm (T1:14; T2:22; T3:55), so G-7 is one goal for everyone. G-4 also includes HW2's G-32, since both are about the same reminder. G-17 and G-43 both help G-16 but in different ways, so we kept them apart. We also had three conflicts. For C1, Rachel wants every flag right away (T2:17, T2:45), but Harold doesn't want her told when he is a few minutes late (T1:46; F1:29). We followed Dr. Nguyen, who said urgency depends on the drug (T3:57, T3:59). High-risk flags go urgently to Harold and Dr. Nguyen, low-risk flags are just a notice to Harold, and we kept G-26 in the model as the option we rejected. For C2, Rachel needs urgent warnings, but she can only be told if Harold agrees (T3:33; F1:23). We treated consent as a hard rule and gave Rachel three levels: nothing by default, safety contact, and sharing per medication. For C3, "missed" meant something different to each person (F2:20; T1:42; T3:70). So each medication has its own window and risk level (G-38). A dose is missed when the window closes, and a duplicate or "unsure" dose is flagged right away. To keep Harold independent (G-31), he only gets one reminder and no flag while he is still inside the window. The goal model really helped with all of this. The AND-refinements showed that G-21 needs G-38, G-22 and G-23 together, or flagging doesn't work. The conflict links made us decide on disagreements before writing the spec, so the AI didn't decide for us. Keeping G-26 in the model also gave us a clear reason for why Rachel isn't told about every flag.

We left some things out on purpose: regimen changes (G-9 vs. G-15), pharmacist advice (G-19) and resolving an unsure dose (G-18), Rachel seeing the full history (HW2 G-28), and real notifications and patient data. These are not part of the double-dose loop, or they need their own regimen feature, so they can wait.

## What we found in V1

When we went through the quickstart, we found 8 problems, and all of them were requirements issues, not code bugs. The code did what the spec said, the spec just had gaps. After Harold says "I'm not sure" about Levothyroxine, FR-008 still tells him to wait, even though there is no record of him taking it, and no one can sort it out because G-18/G-19 are out of scope. After he says "Yes, I took another" at 18:30, FR-009 keeps Metoprolol scheduled, so he gets a reminder at 20:00, which pretty much invites a third dose, and then a missed alert at 21:00 that isn't true. This goes against G-22 and G-16. The card and dialog also don't say which dose they are about. "It has been noted" (FR-017) doesn't say who noted it, and nobody else sees low-risk flags anyway. "Check with Dr. Nguyen" (FR-015) sounds good, but Harold has no way to contact her, and she only gets a small note added to an old alert, so G-20 is only partly covered. FR-021 sent Rachel the same "Please check in with Harold." message twice at 09:00. That follows the spec, but it goes against our C1 "don't alarm" decision. When Harold turned off the safety contact (FR-020), Rachel's old messages disappeared, and the spec never said if that should happen. Also, Step 9 of our quickstart sets values that were already there, so it doesn't prove that saving works. We had to try Atorvastatin at 45 min / high to really check it.

Most of these need a spec change. FR-008 needs different advice when nothing was recorded, FR-017 needs exact wording, the dialog should show which dose it is by its due time, and FR-021 should put alerts from the same time into one message. FR-009 also needs a spec change, but we want to ask Dr. Nguyen first because it is already an open question. Two need input from stakeholders before we change anything: how Harold can actually reach Dr. Nguyen (FR-015), and if Rachel's old messages should stay after the safety contact is turned off (FR-020), which is a consent question for Harold and Dr. Nguyen. Step 9 just needs values that are different from the seed data.

## Reflection

A lot of decisions needed our own judgment and not the AI's. We chose the sprint scope and worked out C1–C3 before there was any spec. After the first spec draft we changed some things ourselves: the three-choice duplicate prompt, unset medications being high risk by default (the draft said low), "Check with Dr. Nguyen" before saving a late high-risk dose, the 2-hour early-dose limit and 6-hour repeat check, and which dose "took another" is about. When /speckit-analyze said FR-011 had no interview evidence and found the twice-daily double-dose gap, we decided how to fix the spec and made a small fix to the constitution (v1.0.1). Checking V1 against what the stakeholders actually want, and not only against the spec, was also our call. For example, FR-009 is "correct" but still invites a third dose.

Prototyping changed how we understood what the stakeholders need. All the tests passed, but the two identical messages, the false 21:00 alert, and "check with Dr. Nguyen" with no way to reach her only showed up when we clicked through the app as Harold and Rachel. Spec-driven development kept the requirements and the code in line. Every FR maps to code and tests in docs/traceability.md, and the test names start with the scenario or FR ID. Since the code matched the spec, every problem pointed back to a requirement, so it was easy to see they were spec issues and not bugs.

If we had to show management that this process works, we would show the traceability matrix (FR → interview evidence → goal → code → test), the /speckit-analyze findings and our fixes in the git history, the 41 passing unit tests and the 23 of 23 walkthrough checks that passed in the browser, and our V1 findings table with 8 requirements issues that the tests didn't catch.
